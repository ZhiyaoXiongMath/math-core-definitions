CORE MATHEMATICAL DEFINITIONS: SELECTION AND GLOBAL RANKING

Companions: prompt.txt, preamble.tex, template.tex.

PURPOSE AND INPUT

Produce an English list of core and important mathematical concept names, ranked
within each user-designated subject. It supplies context for later mathematical
evaluation; a concept's rank is not a theorem-quality score.

The user determines the subject scope. The supplied books and Indexes determine
which candidates have source support. Keep these two roles distinct: a specialist
book does not redefine the subject, and incomplete sources do not justify adding
concepts from general knowledge. Do not move books or impose a fixed taxonomy.

Use the bibliography or manifest to identify the books. Use main.tex only for
bibliographic identification and preserve it. If no manifest is supplied but the
input grouping is clear, make a short inventory of the actual inputs. For uploaded
files, use the user's subject labels; ask only about a genuinely ambiguous grouping.
Do not exclude a listed book merely because its title differs from the subject.

Let INPUT_DIR be the subject folder and CLASS its full name, preserving spaces
and capitalization. With labelled attachments, use a working folder bearing that
label. Save CLASS_core_definitions.tex in INPUT_DIR, replacing CLASS with the actual
name. Process subjects independently. The document title is fixed.

A. CHECK AND READ THE INDEX MATERIAL

A1. Reuse existing Index text, page images and manifests. A conventional location
is .core-definitions-work/indexes with index-manifest.json in its parent folder;
reuse another clear existing layout. Resolve transferred cache paths against the
supplied files, retaining book identity. Use relative paths in new portable records.
An inaccessible historical path does not make a supplied cache unusable.

A2. Check the material actually available: file identity, readable content, declared
page coverage, continuations, columns, subentries and cross-references. Compare a
supplied cache hash when available. A hash match confirms the supplied bytes, not
the correctness of OCR, a previous audit, or completeness against an absent PDF.
Record existing edition differences and coverage gaps; do not replace them with a
blanket passed status.

A3. If source PDFs are available, validate source identity and repair only missing,
stale, unreadable or incomplete Index portions. Inspect only the cover matter,
contents or bookmarks needed to identify the source and locate its Index, and the
Index pages themselves. Extract text or OCR only those pages. Do not read book
bodies, follow Index references into the body, extract whole-book text or obtain
additional books unless the user requests it.

A4. If only Index excerpts or caches are supplied, use them as the actual inputs.
Distinguish present observations from inherited claims about original PDFs or page
images. Do not require absent originals, claim to have verified them, or abandon
readable supplied material because original-file checks cannot be repeated.
Record whether each book has usable complete-as-supplied, partial, unavailable or
unresolved material. Completion of this stage means every input has been accounted
for, not that all missing evidence has been recovered.

A5. Read all usable subject Indexes and concept-bearing notation indexes for every
listed book. Batches are allowed: retain candidate names with their Index locations
as each batch is read, then combine the batches before final selection and ranking.
Do not rely on snippets, arbitrary samples or only familiar books. Bare symbols
without identifiable concept names do not supply candidates. Multiple transcriptions
of one Index are alternative evidence, not additional votes.

A6. Build candidates from the actual Index entries, their parent/subentry structure
and see/see also references. Mathematical knowledge may guide terminology, synonym
recognition and importance, but cannot supply an absent candidate. An old generated
list is not candidate evidence or a default order. Resolve uncertain text from the
supplied Index context or images where available. If its meaning remains unresolved,
leave that candidate out and retain a short factual uncertainty note. If a subject
has no usable Index, report the gap and continue with other subjects.

B. SELECT AT A CONSISTENT LEVEL

B1. Use two internal tiers. Core concepts are the principal objects, structures,
properties or invariants organizing the subject's main theories. Important concepts
have an independent, substantial role in those theories, their major results or
reusable methods. Index presence establishes candidate eligibility, not admission.
A term is not important merely because it is needed for one local argument.

B2. Objects, structures, conditions, invariants, constructions, decompositions,
operators, equations and reusable methods may qualify. Exclude temporary notation,
proof-local labels, isolated auxiliary terms, generic words, bare personal names,
book titles, and theorem or conjecture titles as list items. Classify by mathematical
role: an independently indexed concept or reusable tool may qualify under B1 even
when conventionally stated as a theorem; a named assertion does not become a concept
by shortening or relabelling its title. A theorem, conjecture or named formula alone
is not evidence for a different concept. Do not reject a substantive eponymous
concept merely because its name contains a person's name.

B3. Each specialization or related construction must independently meet B1.
Related but distinct concepts need not be merged, but neither are they automatically
all admitted. Apply the same threshold across books and topics. Necessary generic
background is not automatically core, and specialized concepts are not automatically
unimportant. Do not use a total count, minimum length, book quota or topic quota.

B4. Before ranking, revisit the principal indexed structures and methods for
omissions, and reconsider marginal selections across topics. Check whether similar
roles have been treated consistently. Do not fill a presumed gap with an unsupported
term, or expand the list just to include every indexed variant. Keep only brief
notes for genuinely unresolved or consequential decisions; no item-by-item essay
or exhaustive rejection ledger is required.

C. NORMALIZE WITHOUT CHANGING MEANING

C1. Recover full names from parents, subentries and cross-references. Use standard
English terminology, translating when necessary without printing bilingual pairs.
Retain the object, category or hypothesis needed to identify the indexed concept.
Do not add a more specific qualifier solely because the subject makes it plausible.
A legitimate broad concept name may remain broad; ambiguity and generality are not
the same. If different senses cannot be distinguished from available evidence,
follow A6 rather than guessing or consulting body text.

C2. Merge genuine synonyms and spelling variants within the subject, keeping one
canonical name and the source links already found. Do not merge merely related
constructions, general/special cases, different categories, or concepts equivalent
only under additional hypotheses. Do not broaden an indexed term into a different
structure. If synonymy is uncertain, do not force it. The same concept may appear
in different subject lists.

C3. One item names one concept. Do not bundle independent names, append synonym
catalogues, definitions or explanatory sentences. Ordinary mathematical notation
forming part of a concept name is allowed; formulas stating properties are not.

D. RANK THE WHOLE SUBJECT LIST

D1. Finish admission, naming and deduplication before deciding the final sequence.
Place all core concepts before all important concepts. Within each tier, prioritize
structural centrality to the subject's main theories and major results, the range
of important results organized or connected, and a concept's essential role in
substantial methods. Rank against the user's subject scope, with the source limits
recorded separately.

D2. Cross-book usage may inform judgment, but frequency, Index page counts, number
of mentioning books and repeated editions are not voting weights. Generality,
abstraction, difficulty, fame, name length and elementary status confer no automatic
priority. Importance takes precedence over teaching order, prerequisite order,
book order, alphabetical order and thematic grouping.

D3. Combine topic or book batches and rank globally; do not concatenate their local
lists. Compare across topics and separated parts of the sequence, including later
principal concepts and long runs from one topic. Also check the leading concepts,
comparable neighbors, general/special pairs and the tier boundary. A topic run is
a reason to inspect the order, not an automatic error or a demand to alternate topics.

D4. When no reliable importance distinction remains within a tier, use alphabetical
order of canonical English names. Do not invent numerical scores or assert that
positions separated only by tie-breaking represent a substantive difference. Display
positions are ordinal, are not comparable across subjects, and are not theorem scores.

D5. Keep a concise internal record linking each final rank and name to its tier and
at least one identifiable Index entry: book identifier, cache file and a usable
location, with the parent context where needed. Reuse an adequate existing format;
no prescribed database or schema is required. Preserve source links for known
synonym merges. The record must describe the delivered list, not an earlier draft.
It is factual provenance, not private reasoning or an additional reader document.

E. DOCUMENT AND LATEX CONTRACT

E1. Copy template.tex and replace the sample item with the selected names. Preserve
its class, packages, margins, title, author/date fields, list settings and framing.
The template embeds preamble.tex; do not load that fragment again. When comparing
the pre-document block, normalize line endings and ignore trailing blank lines
only; keep all other content identical. Preserve the companions during generation.

E2. Default to one enumerate with [series=definitions] and no sections. Write one
\item per source line. Display automatic Arabic ranks starting at 1 and remove
the sample Concept name. At most three broad English sections may divide natural
consecutive blocks without moving entries. If sections are used, every section
is followed by one enumerate: [series=definitions] first, then [resume=definitions].
Do not mix sectioned and unsectioned lists or use manual item labels/counter resets.

E3. The final TeX contains only the fixed title, optional section headings, ranks
and English concept names. No explanations, definitions, citations, source pages,
tier labels, scores, notes or reports, including in comments. No subsections,
nested lists, tables, decorations, custom macros, external inputs or new packages.
Encode accents and necessary notation using ordinary LaTeX supported by the supplied
preamble. The complete generated TeX is standalone, with no dependency on this
prompt package, local skill or additional source file.

F. CHECK, SAVE AND DELIVER

F1. Review source support, naming, synonym handling, admission and global order;
then check the internal record against the actual final items. Independently check
template conformity, allowed content, one item per line, no placeholder or external
inputs, section limits and continuous automatic numbering. Use an available checker
when helpful, but do not assume one exists or treat its pass as mathematical proof.

F2. Compile the actual complete output with a standard compiler supporting the
template, such as pdfLaTeX. Select the intended file explicitly when needed and
inspect the result for clipped text, missing glyphs and numbering defects. Do not
compile the preamble, a bibliography or an old output as a substitute. Fix genuine
errors. Distinguish successful checks, checks not performed and checks that failed;
an unavailable compiler is different from a failed compilation. A successful build
does not establish mathematical quality or source completeness.

F3. Preserve source books, Index material, bibliographies and companion files.
Before replacing an existing output, make and verify an unchanged, non-colliding
backup in the working cache. Check a new candidate before replacing an accepted
output; a checked candidate may be compiled under the target basename in a temporary
directory and then copied unchanged. Do not mark a failed candidate as accepted.

F4. Deliver one CLASS_core_definitions.tex per subject. Keep the working Index and
provenance records for reuse without adding them to the reader-facing list. Deliver
a PDF only when requested. Briefly disclose actual source gaps, unresolved material,
known edition differences and checks not completed outside the TeX. A partial source
set may support a qualified result; do not call it complete. Do not claim original
PDF verification, body reading or an end-to-end test that was not performed.
